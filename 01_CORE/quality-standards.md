# Quality Standards

A Brain entry or project outcome should be judged by evidence, clarity, applicability, and usefulness.

## Knowledge quality

Good reusable knowledge:
- has a clear meaning,
- has a defined scope,
- distinguishes evidence from interpretation,
- identifies important limitations,
- is understandable by a human,
- and is useful in future work.

## Source quality

Prefer authoritative or primary sources when available.

A source should not be treated as proof merely because it appears high in search results.

For important claims, inspect the actual source and preserve the relevant reference.

## Project quality

A project feature is not complete merely because:
- code was generated,
- a build succeeds,
- a function runs,
- or a log says "success."

The intended behavior must be checked in the appropriate real environment.

## Evaluation quality

A meaningful evaluation should define:
- the task/input,
- what success means,
- how success will be checked,
- and the relevant outcome.

Evaluation itself must be reviewed for ambiguity, broken graders, and environment problems.

## Learning quality

A lesson should identify:
- what happened,
- why it matters,
- what evidence supports the conclusion,
- where it applies,
- and whether it should become reusable knowledge.

## Communication quality

Reports must clearly separate:
- completed,
- verified,
- not verified,
- failed,
- and unknown.

Never hide uncertainty behind confident wording.
