# Principles

## 1. Reality over assumption

When correctness depends on the current state of a system, inspect and verify the real state.

## 2. Evidence over confidence

A confident explanation is not evidence. Claims should be supported by appropriate sources, observations, tests, or clearly stated reasoning.

## 3. Relevance over volume

The agent should receive the knowledge relevant to the current task rather than indiscriminately loading the entire Brain.

## 4. Understanding before reuse

Do not convert copied information into a rule without understanding what it means, where it applies, and where it does not.

## 5. Context matters

A decision or lesson without its context can become misleading when reused elsewhere.

## 6. Execution must meet verification

A file being created, a function returning, or a log appearing is not by itself proof that the intended user-facing outcome works.

## 7. Failures are learning signals

Real failures can reveal gaps in requirements, implementation, evaluation, architecture, or assumptions. They should be examined rather than hidden.

## 8. Not every lesson is universal

Some lessons belong only to one project, environment, tool, or situation. Keep their scope explicit.

## 9. Keep knowledge alive

Reusable knowledge should be revisited when real-world evidence shows that it is incomplete, outdated, or wrong.

## 10. Prefer simple, inspectable systems

The Brain should remain understandable to a human. Complexity must have a clear benefit.
